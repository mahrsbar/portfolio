    Context
    
          
    
            
    Who asked
    Sales and product, for a prospective customer.
    
            
    What it had to do
    Feel like the real investigation product, show AI in the workflow, and run with no credentials.
    
            
    What it could not do
    Leak real data, or be a chatbot that is confidently wrong. Investigators want the right page, not a paragraph, and one wrong answer poisons every answer after it.
    
            
    How it was built
    Solo, as a small sibling of the product: a React front end, a small back end that proxies the data APIs and holds the keys, and the AI layer. Claude Code wrote most of the code; I reviewed every change that touched data.
    
          
    
          
    So the AI was designed into the journey an analyst already takes, in four moments: ask, land, dig deeper, and when it's wrong.
    

          
    
            Moment 1 of 4
            
    Ask
    
            
    The question becomes the page. The analyst types what they want to see and gets the page, with the filters already set.
    
            
    
              
              
            
    
            
    
              The ask bar never answers. The model returns one flat plan, enforced through structured outputs, and
              the app applies it as the filters the product already has. Anything it can't infer comes back
              null, so the page opens without that filter rather than with a guessed one.
            
    
            
    
              The plan also says what kind of question it was. A navigate question is answered by the page
              itself. An answer question opens the page and the assistant together, with the question
              already asked, so the page frames the evidence while the assistant answers.
            
    
            
    
              The decision
              
    The model is a translator, not an oracle.
    
              
    Its output is the product's own vocabulary. A wrong answer from a chatbot is a trust problem; a wrong filter is just a filter.

              That line isn't generated. A small narrator computes it locally from the rows on screen: shared
              devices, a failure rate, two checks too far apart to be the same person. Nothing is sent to a model,
              and every claim cites the transactions behind it. It uses the map's own impossible-travel thresholds
              and only the visible rows, so it never disagrees with the screen next to it.
            
    
            
    
              The decision
              
    A button that says "Assistant" gets ignored. A specific observation gets clicked.
    
              
    A single truncated sentence looked like debug output. An eyebrow that carries the severity, two lines of text and an arrow made it something that asks to be opened.
    
            
    
          
    

          
    
            Psych note
            
    The cocktail party effect
    
            
    
              In a loud room you can follow one conversation and tune out the rest, yet your own name cuts straight
              through. Selective attention is a filter that lets a few relevant signals in.
            
    
            
    
              A fraud dataset is the loud room. The ask bar hands the investigator one thread, and the launcher
              speaks their name across it: the account they're looking at, and the one thing worth knowing about
              it. The AI never adds to the noise; it only decides which conversation to follow.

              Each step and its one-line result appear as they happen, so you see how it got to the answer. It stops
              after eight tool steps and says what it found. By default it sees only the invented records the pages
              show. Reaching real data takes a deployment setup with a named security sign-off, not a switch.
            
    
            
    
              The decision
              
    Show the steps. Bound the effort. Gate the data in the setup, not the prompt.
    
              
    Watching the steps is how people learn when to trust it. A spinner teaches nothing.

              The two patterns that moved into the roadmap are these rules applied: question-to-filters is
              "fixable" made into a feature, and the gated assistant is "bounded" made into a setup.
            
    
          
    

          
    The outcome
    
          
      
            
    • The reference demo for a major prospect, then reused as the developer portal's embedded walkthrough.
      • 
            
    • Two patterns on the production roadmap: question-to-filters and the gated assistant.
      • 
            
    • A stress test for the component library: every deviation the demo needed became a library ticket rather than a one-off override.
      • 
          
    

          
    What I took from it
    
          
      
            
    • Design from the failure case. What happens when it's wrong shaped every surface more than what happens when it's right.
      • 
            
    • Constrain the AI to the product's vocabulary. Translators are easy to trust and easy to correct.
      • 
            
    • Make safety structural. A rule people could ignore is weaker than a setup that can't send anything real.
