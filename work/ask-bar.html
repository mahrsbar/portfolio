Case 02Fraud-investigation suite

Moving our AI from a chat page onto the maps analysts work in

  • ~6 min → secondsTo get from a question to the filtered map, and again for each follow-up
  • 5 steps → 1Ask on the map. The page, the filters and the first finding are set for you
The problem
Analysts work on a map of geolocation checks, a graph of shared devices and a table of transactions. Our AI lived on a separate chat page. Using it meant going back and forth between the chat and the map, so 9 in 10 never opened it.
What I did
I put the AI on the page. One question opens the right view with its filters set, the page flags the strongest finding when you land, and every answer shows its sources.
My role
I designed and built the first version alone, from the ask bar to the agent loop, and wrote its security design review. I now lead engineering on the assistant and co-lead the product's development.

The problem

Text chat works for narrow lookups and support questions. In an investigation the evidence is on the map and the graph, and a separate chat page turns it into paragraphs to read and check. Even when it draws a chart, you can't hide the passed checks or zoom to one area without asking again. Over 90 days, the chat showed up in 0.3% of sessions.

1. The ask bar

One question opens the right page, already filtered. "Failed checks in Halifax this week" opens the map with Status, Range and Place set, and a wrong filter is a chip you remove in one click.

How one question is answeredFrom the ask bar to the page, as a diagram

2. The insight

It flags the strongest finding on the page before you ask. Open it and every point lists the transactions it came from.

The first insight was a status line that repeated a count the page already showed, so there was no reason to open it. It now leads with something the page doesn't show, like a phone shared by two accounts, with a label for how serious it is.

The read behind it was first written by a model from the page's rows. I replaced it the same day with a narrator that runs in the browser, with no model call, so no transaction data leaves the app. It uses the map's own impossible-travel thresholds and only the rows on screen, so it can't disagree with the page next to it.

3. The assistant

It opens from the top bar on any page, for questions a page can't answer, and shows its sources and its limits.

Before it could ship, I asked what every part does when the model gets it wrong. Three rules came out of it: a mistake is one click to undo, it shows what it doesn't know, and hard limits are built in rather than left to the prompt. The security design review was written from them.

It works through the account with tools while you watch. Every fact comes from a tool call to an internal MCP server, made as the analyst: each call carries their verified email and organisation, so the server limits the answer to the companies they can see.

Assistant

Why does user-maya keep failing?

What broke in testing, and what changed

  • The insight raised false alarms. It counted one account typed with different capitals as three accounts sharing a phone, and read a missing location as a 5,000-mile trip. It now ignores capitals and blank locations, and only calls travel impossible when it passes the same distance and speed limits the map uses.
  • The assistant took the map away from you. If you zoomed in by hand and asked a follow-up, the answer reset the map to its own view. Your zoom now stays unless the question names a new place.
  • The assistant slowed itself down. Before answering, it looked up each of the analyst's companies one by one, even though the app already had their names. The app now sends those names with the question, and on the same 25 test questions answers got 25% faster.
The lesson

When people work in maps and graphs, let the AI open the right one, set its filters and point to the finding that matters.

A reply in a chat has to be read, then checked against the data. On a filtered map with one finding and its sources, the analyst can check it at a glance.
