    The brief
    
          
    
            Sales and product asked for a demo for a prospective customer: something that felt like the real
            investigation product, showed AI in the workflow, and ran with no credentials. Two things it could not
            do: leak real data, or be a chatbot that is confidently wrong. Investigators want the right page, not a
            paragraph, and one wrong answer poisons every answer after it.
          
    
          
    
            So the AI went into the four moments an analyst already passes through:
            ask, land, dig deeper, and
            when it's wrong.
          
    

          
    
            Moment 1 of 4
            
    Ask
    
            
    The question becomes the page. The analyst types what they want to see and gets the page, with the filters already set.
    
            
    
              
              
            
    
            
    
              The ask bar never answers. The model returns one flat plan, enforced through structured outputs, and
              the app applies it as the filters the product already has. Anything it can't infer comes back
              null, so the page opens without that filter, never with a guessed one.
            
    
            
    
              The plan also says what kind of question it was. A navigate question is answered by the page
              itself. An answer question opens the page and the assistant together, with the question
              already asked, so the page frames the evidence while the assistant answers.
            
    
            
    
              The decision
              
    The model returns filters, never an answer.
    
              
    Its output is the product's own vocabulary. A wrong answer from a chatbot is a trust problem. A wrong filter is one click to remove.

              That line isn't generated. A small narrator computes it locally from the rows on screen: shared
              devices, a failure rate, two checks too far apart to be the same person. Nothing is sent to a model,
              and every claim cites the transactions behind it. It uses the map's own impossible-travel thresholds
              and only the visible rows, so it never disagrees with the screen next to it.
            
    
            
    
              The decision
              
    A button that says "Assistant" gets ignored. A specific observation gets clicked.
    
              
    A single truncated sentence looked like debug output. An eyebrow that carries the severity, two lines of text and an arrow made it something that asks to be opened.
    
            
    
          
    

          
    
            Psych note
            
    The cocktail party effect
    
            
    
              In a loud room you can follow one conversation and tune out the rest, yet your own name cuts straight
              through. Selective attention is a filter that lets a few relevant signals in.
            
    
            
    
              A fraud dataset is the loud room. The launcher says the investigator's name across it: the account
              they're looking at, and the one thing worth knowing about it.
            
    
            Cherry, 1953; Moray, 1959
          
    

          
    
            Moment 3 of 4
            
    Dig deeper
    
            
    An assistant that shows its work. For questions a page can't answer, it works through the account with tools while you watch.

    A recreated mockup with invented data.
    
            
    
              Each step and its one-line result appear as they happen, so you see how it got to the answer. It stops
              after eight tool steps and says what it found. Every fact in the answer comes from a tool call to an
              internal MCP server, made as the analyst, so it reaches only the records they are permitted to see. By default it sees only the invented records the pages
              show. Reaching real data takes a deployment setup with a named security sign-off, not a switch.
            
    
            
            
    The shape of the system, redrawn without the product’s names.
    
            
    
              The decision
              
    Show the steps. Bound the effort. Gate the data in the setup, not the prompt.
    
              
    Watching the steps is how people learn when to trust it. A spinner teaches nothing.

              The two patterns that moved into the roadmap are these rules applied: question-to-filters is
              "fixable" made into a feature, and the gated assistant is "bounded" made into a setup.
            
    
          
    

          
    The outcome
    
          
    
            Two of the demo's patterns went onto the product roadmap and have since been built into the product: the
            question that becomes filters, and an assistant that can only reach what the analyst can. And every
            place the demo had to deviate from the component library became a
            library ticket instead of a one-off override.
          
    
          
    
            Design from the failure case. What happens when it's wrong shaped every surface more
            than what happens when it's right.
